During my recent trip to the Maldives, I had the pleasure of staying at the **DusitD2 Feydhoo Maldives**, a luxurious resort offering overwater villas, all-inclusive dining, and a range of activities from snorkeling to nightly entertainment. Here's a detailed account of my experience.

## Arrival & Transfer

After landing at the airport, the resort arranged a **seamless transfer via ferry**. The journey across the crystal-clear waters of the Maldives was breathtaking and set the tone for the luxurious experience awaiting us.

- **Airport Arrival:** Smooth and welcoming.
- **Ferry Transfer:** Approximately 25 minutes across the lagoon, offering stunning views of the ocean and nearby islands.

The check-in process was effortless, with staff ensuring every detail was attended to. Our overwater villa was nothing short of spectacular:

- **Private deck with direct lagoon access** – perfect for a morning swim.
- **Spacious interiors** with modern amenities and a minimalist, elegant design.
- **Panoramic views** of the turquoise waters and nearby islands.

## Our verdict

Let's be direct: **DusitD2 Feydhoo is the resort you pick when you want the Maldives without the logistics headache.** The 25-minute ferry transfer is the whole ballgame here. Most Maldives trips start with an agonizing choice — pay for a seaplane (weather-dependent, limited daytime schedules, no night flights) or an expensive domestic flight plus another boat. Feydhoo skips all of that. You land, you step onto a boat, and you're at the resort in time for a sunset swim. For a family trip, where the journey is already stressful, that simplicity is worth more than any glossy brochure amenity.

Who is this resort for? **Families with kids old enough to swim confidently** — the shallow lagoon is a natural playground, and the shallow-water snorkeling right off your villa deck means you don't need to book boat excursions to see fish. **Couples who want all-inclusive ease** without the party-resort chaos. And frankly, **anyone who's been burned by mediocre Caribbean all-inclusive food** — the dining here is a different league entirely, closer to city fine-dining than resort buffet.

Who should skip it? If your dream is the true castaway experience — one tiny island, nothing but ocean in every direction, total disconnection — this isn't that. Feydhoo is relatively close to Malé, and you'll see other islands on the horizon. It's also not the resort for travelers who need a big brand name for the brag factor; DusitD2 is a well-run brand but doesn't carry the same cachet as the Sonevas and Four Seasons of the Maldives. You get comparable luxury at what is typically a much saner price point — just don't book it expecting the "Instagram elite" crowd.

The value case is what sealed it for us. In the Maldives, overwater villas routinely run into four figures a night at the big-name resorts. An all-inclusive package with five genuinely good restaurants, plus the activities baked in, makes the math easy: you know your spend before you go, and the quality never once felt like "included" food.

## What we'd do differently

Honestly, there's not much we'd change — but a few things we'd optimize next time:

**Pick a villa closer to the reef edge, not the jetty end.** Our overwater villa was spectacular, but the water near the start of the jetties is shallower and murkier. Walk a bit further out and the coral starts to come alive. When you check in, it's worth asking which villas have the best direct snorkeling access — staff know exactly which stretch of reef is liveliest that week.

**Don't wait until day two to grab snorkeling gear.** We almost skipped it, assuming we'd do it "later." The house reef is the resort's best asset and it's free the moment you have a mask. Get the gear on day one — even a lazy afternoon drift off your own deck is better than most paid excursions we've done elsewhere.

**Plan one restaurant per night and don't default to the buffet.** It's tempting to fall into the rhythm of the main dinner buffet (it's good!), but Soi Thai and Bo Chinese were genuine highlights. We'd have been better off with a simple rotation: one themed restaurant each night, buffet only on arrival day when you're tired.

**Wake up for sunrise.** We mean this. The lagoon at sunrise — flat water, no one else on the decks, the whole thing bathed in warm light — was one of the quietest moments of the trip. The villa deck faces make early mornings magical, and you'll beat the heat of the day for that first swim.

**Leave buffer time on departure day.** The 25-minute ferry sounds trivial, but Maldives logistics — check-out, ferry scheduling, Malé traffic, airport security — always take longer than you expect. Don't book a tight connection.

## Practical tips

A few things worth knowing before you book:

**Transfers are easy, but confirm the details in advance.** The ferry runs from the airport area, so you don't need the seaplane drama. That said, confirm your transfer time with the resort before you fly — late-night arrivals can mean a wait, and you'll want to know exactly where to meet the staff at the airport.

**Choose the overwater villa if it's within reach.** This is the signature experience, and the lagoon-access deck is what makes it: morning swims straight off your steps, fish visible from your sun lounger. Beach villas are lovely too, but the overwater villa is why you came to this resort.

**All-inclusive means all-inclusive — so use it.** Five restaurants, live cooking stations, high-end buffets: eat widely and don't ration yourself. The dessert station alone justified the package for us. Note that activities like the fun tube ($55 per person) are extras, so budget a little on top for the paid watersports.

**Pack for the sun, not for the evening.** You'll live in swimwear. Bring reef-safe sunscreen (the lagoon life you're here to see is exactly what regular sunscreen damages), a rash guard for snorkeling days, and water shoes — shallow lagoons sometimes hide coral rubble. Evenings are casual; there's no dress-code pressure here.

**August worked, but know the trade-offs.** We visited in August and had a fantastic time — warm water, quiet lagoon. The Maldives' southwest monsoon season can bring brief rain showers, though we found it mostly passed quickly. If you need guaranteed cloudless skies, the dry season (roughly November–April) is the classic choice, but expect busier resorts and higher rates.

**Book directly when you can, and ask about meal-plan upgrades.** Resort websites often bundle the all-inclusive plan with transfers at a cleaner price than third-party booking sites, which sometimes list room-only rates that mislead you on the true cost. Ask what the package includes (the $55/person watersports clearly didn't — confirm what else is extra) so there are no surprises at check-out.
