# Sicily in 5 Days: A Fast-Paced Itinerary From Palermo to Taormina

{{< youtube iXPtSY4PsPE >}}

Sicily is Italy's - and the Mediterranean's - largest island, and there's no realistic way to see all of it in 5 days. What you can do is hit a genuinely great cross-section of it: a Norman cathedral town, a clifftop medieval village, a UNESCO valley of Greek temples, a baroque island city, an active volcano, and a cliffside resort town. This is the fast-paced route we ran, day by day, with the practical details you'd want if you're building a similar trip.

## Day 1: Palermo and Monreale

We flew into Palermo, in the northwest corner of the island, and picked up a rental car right at the airport.

![Arrival at Palermo Airport](palermo-arrival.jpg)

The drive from the airport up to **Monreale takes about 30 minutes**, and it's worth doing right away - we chose to base ourselves in an apartment in a Palermo neighborhood rather than Monreale itself, which worked fine given the short drive between the two.

**Monreale Cathedral is one of the most famous churches in all of Sicily**, and the hilltop town it sits in is worth the trip on its own for the views back down over Palermo.

![Monreale Cathedral and the hilltop town around it](monreale-cathedral.jpg)

For dinner, look for one of the many hilltop restaurants - we had **Palermo city glittering down below** us as the sun went down, and it turned into one of the best dinner views of the whole trip.

![Palermo city lit up at night, seen from a hilltop restaurant](palermo-hilltop-dinner.jpg)

If you're an early riser, a **gorgeous sunrise over the harbour** the next morning made getting up worth it.

![Sunrise over the Palermo harbour and surrounding hills](palermo-sunrise.jpg)

## Day 2: Erice

Erice is a clifftop medieval village above the port city of Trapani, and it's a full day trip from the Palermo area - budget a good chunk of the day for the winding roads up.

You have two options for the final ascent: **drive up to the village directly**, or **take the cable car (the Funivia Erice)** from Trapani. Tickets for the cable car can be **bought online in advance** to skip the line.

![The Funivia Erice cable car ticket and information board](erice-funivia.jpg)

From the cable car, you get **gorgeous views over Trapani and the Mediterranean** on the way up.

![View over Trapani and the Mediterranean from the Erice cable car](erice-cablecar-view.jpg)

Once you're up top, plan on walking the whole village - it's compact, but the views are **for miles, so bring a hat and sunglasses**.

![Walkway along Erice's medieval castle walls](erice-castle-walk.jpg)

One thing to know: Erice sits high enough that it's **incredibly scenic with low clouds rolling in and out** through the day, which can change the whole mood of your visit in minutes.

![Low clouds rolling into the valley below Erice](erice-clouds.jpg)

## Day 3: Agrigento and the Valley of the Temples

From the Palermo/Erice side of the island, it's a multi-hour drive south to Agrigento - budget most of a driving day for this leg.

We checked into the **Dimora B&B**, which has direct **temple views** from the property itself - a genuinely special place to stay given what's next door.

![The Dimora B&B, with a Greek temple visible through the trees](agrigento-dimora-bb.jpg)

Nearby, **Borgo Santuli has a nice beach that's swimmable at sunset** if you want to cool off before or after the temples.

![Aerial view of the coastline near Borgo Santuli beach](agrigento-beach.jpg)

**The Valle dei Templi is Agrigento's main attraction**, and it deserves the morning or a full half-day. Plan to walk the whole site rather than just seeing it from a viewpoint.

![The Valle dei Templi at golden hour](valle-dei-templi.jpg)

The classic route is to **walk down from the Tempio di Giunone to the Temple of Concordia**, one of the best-preserved Greek temples anywhere.

![Walking path down toward the Temple of Concordia](temple-walk.jpg)

Just below Concordia, don't miss **the famous fallen Icarus** - a striking bronze statue lying in the dirt in front of the temple.

![The fallen Icarus statue in front of the Temple of Concordia](fallen-icarus.jpg)

Finish the site by continuing down to the **Temple of Heracles**, where you'll exit back to the parking area.

## Day 3 (evening): On to Siracusa, via Modica

The drive from Agrigento to Siracusa is a long one, so break it up with lunch around Ragusa or San Giovanni. If your route passes through **Modica, stop at the town's famous chocolate shop, Antica Dolceria Bonajuto** - it's the oldest chocolate factory in Sicily and well worth the detour.

![The historic Antica Dolceria Bonajuto chocolate shop in Modica](modica-chocolate.jpg)

We stayed at the **Ortea Palace Hotel, on Ortigia Island** - the historic heart of Siracusa - in a **palatial, luxurious room** with a marina view.

![Marina view from a palatial room at the Ortea Palace Hotel](siracusa-room-view.jpg)

## Day 4: Siracusa and Ortigia

Siracusa is worth a proper day. **Catch sunset at one of the many beaches around the city**, then spend time wandering **Ortigia**, the small island that makes up the old town.

![Sunset over Ortigia's skyline](siracusa-sunset.jpg)

From above, you can really see why the **Ortea Palace Hotel's location on Ortigia Island** is such a good base - it's surrounded by water on nearly every side.

![Aerial view of Ortigia Island and its harbor](ortea-palace-hotel.jpg)

At the tip of the island, **Ortigia's castle (Castello Maniace) also has its own Icarus statue** in the courtyard, echoing the one in Agrigento.

![Castello Maniace at the tip of Ortigia Island](ortigia-castle-icarus.jpg)

## Day 5: Mount Etna and Taormina

From Siracusa, **head out to Mount Etna, towering over Sicily** - it's the largest active volcano in Europe, and driving up its slopes is a highlight on its own.

Buy your **cable car (Funivia dell'Etna) tickets** for the ride up toward the summit craters. As you climb, **the landscape turns lunar - covered in ash all over**, a striking contrast to the green coastline below.

![The Etna cable car climbing through the ash-covered landscape](etna-cablecar-lunar.jpg)

Etna is often wrapped in cloud, but **as the clouds clear you can see all the way down to Catania** on the coast.

![View down to Catania from near Etna's summit](etna-summit-catania.jpg)

Descend Etna and head north to **Taormina** - our tip from the trip is that it's **worth spending two nights in Etna's backyard** here rather than rushing through, even on a fast-paced itinerary like this one. Taormina's rooftops look right across the bay at Etna itself.

![Taormina's rooftops with Mount Etna visible across the bay](taormina-rooftops-etna.jpg)

In the evening, Taormina's **cobblestone pedestrian stretch has genuinely pretty architecture** and **lots of great restaurants to choose from** - we happened to catch fireworks over the main piazza.

![Taormina's main piazza at night, with fireworks in the background](taormina-piazza-night.jpg)

For beach time, **have your hotel book you a spot at Lido La Pigna** - a beach club with a cable car down from town and a shuttle to bring you back up. **After a fast-paced few days, this is where you relax in crystal-clear water.**

![Lido La Pigna beach club below Taormina](lido-la-pigna.jpg)

![Crystal-clear water at Lido La Pigna](crystal-clear-waters.jpg)

Before dinner, visit Taormina's **well-preserved Greek theater** - the views from the seats, looking out over the coastline toward Etna, are some of the best on the whole trip.

![Aerial view of Taormina and its Greek theater, with the coastline stretching toward Naxos](taormina-greek-theater.jpg)

## Wrapping Up

That's Sicily in 5 fast-paced days. We continued on to the Italian mainland via the ferry crossing at Messina, but Sicily alone easily justifies the whole trip.

![Departing Sicily via Messina, with the Madonna della Lettera statue at the harbor](messina-departure.jpg)

## If You're Planning This Yourself

A few practical notes for anyone building a similar route:

- **Rent a car.** This itinerary only works with your own wheels - Monreale, Erice, Agrigento, and Etna are all much harder to reach on public transport on this kind of timeline.
- **Book cable car tickets online in advance** for both Erice (Funivia Erice) and Etna (Funivia dell'Etna) to skip the lines.
- **This pace is genuinely fast.** If you have more than 5 days, our biggest recommendation is to add a second night in Taormina rather than compressing it into one.
- **Bring a hat and sunglasses** for Erice, and pack for both heat (the temples, the coast) and cooler, windier conditions (Etna's higher elevations).
